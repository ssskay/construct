# Research Brief: Prior-art and substrate validation of the condition-driven participation pattern (cdsp.82)

- **Date**: 2026-07-09
- **Author**: cx-researcher (Construct persona)

## Question

Does the `construct-pteo2` condition-driven participation design — as decided in ADR-0070's `participationRules` schema and recruit/collaborate/execute/enforce pipeline — align with (a) this repo's own prior architectural decisions on registries, frameworks, and org authoring, and (b) published multi-agent orchestration prior art on dynamic team composition and role assignment? Where does it diverge, and what open questions does that divergence raise for the epic's remaining children?

## Findings

### Finding 1: ADR-0070 correctly extends ADR-0046's registry model rather than forking it

**Observation**: ADR-0070 declares `participationRules` as "registry-declared data in exactly lyxx's sense — it lives on a specialist or team's registry entry (or a project overlay), resolved at the same `loadRegistry()` boundary, not a new hardcoded constant" (`docs/decisions/adr/0070-participation-pipeline-and-rules-schema.md:22`). ADR-0046 established that modular-file, runtime-merged shape: "`lib/registry/loader.mjs` `assembleRegistry(rootDir)` walks the tree, merges `.cx/org/` overlay, validates, and caches by directory mtime. No committed monolithic JSON is the source of truth" (`docs/decisions/adr/0046-modular-org-runtime-merge.md:22`).
**Inference**: The two ADRs are consistent by construction — ADR-0070 does not introduce a second registry-resolution boundary or a competing storage location, it adds one new attachable field (`participationRules`) to an entry type ADR-0046 already made runtime-mergeable. This is architecturally the cheapest possible extension: no new loader, no new merge-precedence rule.
**Confidence**: high — both ADRs are primary sources, read in full, and the specific mechanism (`loadRegistry()`/`assembleRegistry()`) is named identically in both.
**Sources**: ADR-0070, ADR-0046.

### Finding 2: ADR-0070 and ADR-0062 target different axes and do not conflict, but neither ADR states how a recruited specialist's `role` (author/reviewer/advisor) interacts with a `framework`-equipped specialist's reasoning steps

**Observation**: ADR-0062 defines a `framework` as an ordered, labeled reasoning procedure bound to a specialist via a `framework: <framework-id>` field on the specialist's prompt frontmatter, and states explicitly that "Frameworks and role skills stay separate concerns" from the specialist's identity (`docs/decisions/adr/0062-lmcp-f6-persona-reasoning-framework-format.md:68`). ADR-0070's `Execute` stage says "a skill or role-flavor (`classifyRoleFlavors`/`roleFlavors` in `lib/orchestration/flow-selection.mjs:260-278`, already live) adapts *how* it executes without changing *who* is recruited or *what* role they hold" (`docs/decisions/adr/0070-participation-pipeline-and-rules-schema.md:47`) — naming role-flavors, not frameworks, as the execution-adaptation mechanism.
**Inference**: The two mechanisms are compatible in principle (both are "adapt how, not who/what role" claims), but ADR-0070's Execute stage never actually names ADR-0062's `framework` field, only `roleFlavors`. A specialist recruited into an `advisor` role via a `participationRules` entry and a specialist equipped with a persona reasoning framework are two independent bindings on the same specialist record with no stated interaction — it is not established in either ADR whether a recruited `advisor`'s output is expected to carry the framework's `emits` fingerprint (per ADR-0062 §3) the same way an `author`'s would, or whether advisory-role output has a lighter contract.
**Confidence**: medium — this is an inference about an unstated interaction, not a direct contradiction; both ADRs are individually clear about their own scope, so the gap is an absence of cross-reference rather than an error in either document.
**Sources**: ADR-0070, ADR-0062.

### Finding 5: ADR-0072's `org-api` CRUD surface and ADR-0070's `participationRules` schema are additive, not competing, but neither ADR names the other

**Observation**: ADR-0072 scopes itself to "a typed CRUD façade over the existing storage and validation layers... no new file format, no new storage location, no new validation engine" for specialists, teams, contracts, fences, and skills (`docs/decisions/adr/0072-no-code-org-authoring-api.md:40`), and lists its `kind` enum as `'specialist' | 'team' | 'contract' | 'fence' | 'skill'` — `participationRules` is not one of the five kinds ADR-0072 enumerates. ADR-0070 in turn never mentions `org-api`, `createEntity`, or a CRUD/authoring surface at all; its own §"cdsp.60/.61" forward-references ("Org Studio: participation-rules canvas," "CLI/MCP parity for participation rules") are the epic's own children, not ADR-0072.
**Inference**: The two designs are compatible on paper (both sit on `assembleRegistry()`/registry-declared JSON, per Finding 1's mechanism), but ADR-0072's CRUD surface as literally specified does not yet cover writing a `participationRules` array — a future `cdsp.60`/`cdsp.61` implementation (Org Studio canvas, CLI/MCP parity for participation rules, both currently `○ OPEN` per the epic) would need either an ADR-0072 amendment adding `participationRules` as a sixth `kind`, or a field-level extension to `updateEntity('specialist'|'team', id, { participationRules: [...] })` under ADR-0072's existing patch semantics. Neither path is decided by either ADR as written.
**Confidence**: medium — this is a structural reading of both ADRs' explicit scopes, not a claim about implementation code (ADR-0072's `org-api.mjs` module does not exist on any branch's `lib/` yet per Finding 4's ancestry check, so there is no code to inspect for how — or whether — this gap was later closed).
**Sources**: ADR-0072; ADR-0070.

### Finding 6: External multi-agent orchestration frameworks converge on the same three-stage shape ADR-0070 formalizes as recruit → collaborate/execute → enforce, but differ sharply on where the "who joins" decision is centralized

**Observation**: AutoGen's `SelectorGroupChat` centralizes next-participant selection in a "Group Chat Manager agent [that] uses an LLM-based algorithm to dynamically choose the next participant based on conversation context," while its own documentation notes that "simple rules are sufficient and more reliable" as an alternative to LLM-based selection (https://microsoft.github.io/autogen/stable//user-guide/core-user-guide/design-patterns/group-chat.html, fetched 2026-07-09). CrewAI's hierarchical process assigns work through "a manager agent [that] allocates tasks to agents based on their capabilities, reviews outputs, and assesses task completion" rather than a fixed chain (https://docs.crewai.com/en/concepts/processes, fetched 2026-07-09). LangChain's "Handoffs" pattern is explicitly decentralized: "Tool calls update a state variable that triggers routing or configuration changes, switching agents," with individual agents (not a central coordinator) invoking the handoff (https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langchain/multi-agent, fetched 2026-07-09).
**Inference**: ADR-0070's `when(signals) matches` recruit stage (a declarative condition evaluated centrally against `requestSignals`, per the ADR's own mermaid diagram) is architecturally closest to AutoGen's rule-based selection mode (deterministic predicate over shared context) and CrewAI's manager-delegated model (centralized allocation decision), and furthest from LangChain's decentralized Handoffs pattern (where an already-active agent, not a central evaluator, decides to hand off). This is a real design choice ADR-0070 makes implicitly rather than by naming the alternative it rejected: a decentralized model where a currently-executing specialist could recruit a peer mid-run (analogous to Handoffs) is not the shape `participationRules`' `when`/`recruit` stage takes — recruitment in ADR-0070 is always evaluated against `requestSignals` at the three named insertion points, not invoked by a specialist already in flight.
**Confidence**: high for the factual comparison (all three framework claims are independently fetched and quoted); medium for the inference about which alternative ADR-0070 implicitly rejected, since ADR-0070's own "Rejected alternatives" section does not discuss a decentralized/peer-invoked recruitment model at all — this is this brief's own structural comparison, not a claim ADR-0070 makes about itself.
**Sources**: AutoGen Group Chat docs; CrewAI Processes docs; LangChain Multi-agent docs; ADR-0070.

### Finding 7: The closest academic analogue to ADR-0070's "recruit" stage (DyLAN) recruits by a learned fitness score, not a declarative rule — a difference in mechanism ADR-0070 does not need to resolve, but should be aware of if it later scores candidates rather than matching conditions

**Observation**: DyLAN "operates a two-stage paradigm of Team Optimization and Task Solving," where Team Optimization "automatically selects a team of agents from candidates to collaborate... using an unsupervised metric called Agent Importance Score" computed from a preliminary trial run, with reported gains "up to 25.0%" on certain MMLU subjects (https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02170, fetched 2026-07-09). ADR-0070's `Recruit` stage matches `participationRules[].when` — a `watchCondition` name or `signalExpr` — against `requestSignals`, a fixed, declarative, non-learned evaluation (`docs/decisions/adr/0070-participation-pipeline-and-rules-schema.md:45`).
**Inference**: ADR-0070's recruit mechanism is deterministic-rule-based, not fitness-scored; DyLAN's is the opposite (a scored, trial-run-informed selection). This is a legitimate, load-bearing design choice for Construct's context: ADR-0070's own "Rationale" section argues for validation-time restrictions ("a validation-time restriction is strictly more reliable than a convention a future author could miss under pressure," `docs/decisions/adr/0070-participation-pipeline-and-rules-schema.md:64`), and a declarative `when` condition is exactly that — a rule a JSON Schema can validate, unlike a learned score. The two approaches are not directly comparable on "which is better" from this evidence; they optimize for different things (DyLAN for measured task performance in a benchmark setting with a trial-run budget; ADR-0070 for auditability, determinism, and schema-validatable governance in a production tool where non-reproducible LLM-scored recruitment would undercut the same ADR's stated advisory/enforced gate distinction).
**Confidence**: medium — the factual claims about each mechanism are high-confidence (directly quoted), but the comparative framing ("which optimizes for what") is this brief's synthesis, not a claim either source makes about the other.
**Sources**: DyLAN (arXiv:2310.02170); ADR-0070.

### Finding 8: The "Embodied LLM Agents" paper's three-part organizational-structure model (topology, role assignment, rules) maps loosely onto ADR-0070's hierarchy semantics, but its role-reassignment loop has no analogue in ADR-0070

**Observation**: The paper defines organizational structure as topology (e.g., "decentralized, centralized with one specific leader, or pyramid"), role assignment ("Multiple leaders with different roles are also allowed"), and rules (e.g., "If the leader's instructions are not right, you can correct the leader"), and describes a "Criticize-Reflect framework" in which "the Coordinator proposes a new organizational prompt" after each evaluation cycle, reassigning roles based on a Critic's evaluation of "agent performance and leadership qualities" (https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12482v2, fetched 2026-07-09). ADR-0070's hierarchy semantics are static within a run: "a **group**... owns decision rights and lists the **squads**... under it; a squad collaborates... a **specialist**... executes the work" (`docs/decisions/adr/0070-participation-pipeline-and-rules-schema.md:50`) — there is no analogue to the paper's post-hoc Criticize-Reflect reassignment loop; ADR-0070's own Consequences section defers "collaboration / join-leave (swarm)" behavior entirely to a future bead (`construct-pteo2.11`, cdsp.40, currently `○ OPEN` per the epic).
**Inference**: ADR-0070's topology (group owns rights, squad collaborates, specialist executes) is a fixed pyramid in the paper's taxonomy, not a topology the pipeline itself can change at runtime — which is consistent with the epic's own phasing (cdsp.40 "join/leave as signals evolve" is explicitly future work, not part of ADR-0070's scope). The paper's evidence is therefore not a counter-argument to ADR-0070 as scoped, but it is directly relevant prior art for cdsp.40 specifically: a working example of a mechanism (Critic-scored role reassignment) that the epic's own "in and out, configurable" framing gestures at but ADR-0070 explicitly does not implement.
**Confidence**: medium — the mapping between the paper's taxonomy and ADR-0070's hierarchy is this brief's structural comparison; the underlying factual claims about each source are high-confidence.
**Sources**: "Embodied LLM Agents Learn to Cooperate in Organized Teams" (arXiv:2403.12482); ADR-0070.

## Counter-evidence

The strongest evidence against treating ADR-0070 as fully validated by this repo's own history is Finding 4: the epic's own commissioning bead assumed ADR-0072 would be found at that number on `staging` and asked this brief to check that assumption, which is itself a signal that a prior planning step (likely the epic's own authoring pass) already expected org-api-related work to be closer to landed than the cdsp.01 substrate audit or this brief's independent re-check confirm it is. This was actively searched for (not merely absent from a first look): `docs/decisions/adr/meta.json` was read directly, the adr directory was listed, and a full `git log --all --grep` sweep was run before concluding ADR-0072 is real but unmerged rather than nonexistent. No counter-evidence was found against Findings 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, or 8 beyond the "medium confidence" caveats already stated inline for the inference portions of each.

## Confidence summary

High confidence: ADR-0070 is architecturally consistent with ADR-0046's registry model (Finding 1); ADR-0070's cited insertion points are real and currently unwired, matching its own claims with only cosmetic line-number drift (Finding 3); ADR-0072 exists but is absent from `staging` under a colliding number scheme (Finding 4); the three external framework comparisons are accurately quoted (Finding 6, factual portion). Medium confidence: the unstated interaction between recruited `role` and ADR-0062 `framework` output contracts (Finding 2); the ADR-0072/ADR-0070 gap on whether `participationRules` is a sixth `org-api` `kind` (Finding 5); the comparative framing against DyLAN and the Embodied LLM Agents paper (Findings 7, 8), which are this brief's synthesis rather than either source's own claim. The single fact most likely to change this brief's conclusions is whether a `construct-pteo2` child bead already resolves Finding 2 or Finding 5's gaps in a way not yet visible in the two ADRs as written — this brief did not read every one of the epic's 21 children in full, only the ones the bead named plus the ones directly cited by ADR-0070 and the substrate audit.

- **Whether any `construct-pteo2` child other than the ones named in this bead's description already closes Finding 2 or Finding 5's gap was not checked.** Only `construct-pteo2` (epic), `construct-pteo2.1` (cdsp.01, read in full), and `construct-pteo2.21` (this bead) were read via `bd show`; the other 19 children's full descriptions were not individually read.

Findings 6-8 are informational context for `construct-pteo2.5` (cdsp.20, generalizing the Oracle swarm recruiter) and `construct-pteo2.11` (cdsp.40, collaboration/join-leave): they show ADR-0070's chosen recruitment shape (centralized, declarative, non-learned) is a defensible but not the only point in the design space external prior art occupies, and that a working example of runtime role reassignment (Finding 8) exists in the literature for whoever scopes cdsp.40's "join/leave as signals evolve" requirement.

## Recommendation

Proceed with ADR-0070 as decided — it is internally consistent with ADR-0046 (Finding 1), its cited insertion points are real (Finding 3), and its recruitment shape is a defensible, well-precedented point in the external design space (Finding 6) rather than an outlier. Before `construct-pteo2.15`/`.16` (Org Studio/CLI-MCP parity) begin implementation work, resolve two things this brief surfaced and ADR-0070/ADR-0072 do not: (1) the ADR-number collision in Finding 4, and (2) whether `participationRules` becomes a sixth `org-api` CRUD `kind` or a patch field on the existing `specialist`/`team` kinds (Finding 5). This recommendation would flip if a full read of ADR-0072's implementation status (once merged) shows `participationRules` write support was already added to `org-api.mjs` after this ADR's `ff1803b0` snapshot — this brief only read the ADR text at that one commit, not any later commit on that branch.

- When the `refactor/consolidate-project-config-dir` branch is reconciled onto `staging`, which side's `0070`/`0071` numbers move — does `staging` keep its current numbers and the incoming branch's ADRs get renumbered `0072`+, or is a different resolution chosen? (Finding 4)

(Finding 7)
